# Destination alert stages

Implemented in source, 16 November 2026. A newly built native app is required for the phone-sound and vibration changes. This document does not establish device acceptance.

Create/Edit Alarm now contains **Repeat alerts**: independent 200 m, 500 m, 1 km and 2 km approach selections, **At my destination**, **May have passed my stop**, and Android **Vibrate with alerts**. At least one alert must remain selected. New alarms default to one 500 m reminder plus arrival and possible-missed checks, with vibration allowed; they remain off until enabled. Saving edits stops the current session as before.

Each selected approach reminder fires once per armed trip after two usable readings. If Android batches readings or GPS jumps past multiple thresholds, the closest useful stage replaces obsolete undelivered reminders; it does not produce a burst of old alarms. A fresh armed session resets the stage history. Existing saved radii become their approach selection without moving saved coordinates or re-enabling completed alarms.

Arrival requires two fresh readings whose reported accuracy circles fit inside the 100 m destination area. This means the **destination area**, not proof of a train platform or a safe place to get off. Arming while already in that area waits for departure beyond 100 m plus an uncertainty/hysteresis margin. Choosing a wide approach threshold does not expand this departure guard or suppress short rides.

A possible missed stop requires two confident close readings within 200 m, then at least three increasing-distance readings over at least ten seconds. The end of the trend must be at least 150 m beyond the closest uncertainty bound, with uncertainty-adjusted radial movement of at least 3 m/s. A gap longer than 30 seconds resets the close/away evidence. A single jump, ordinary slow walking, weak GPS, duplicate fixes and stale fixes cannot satisfy the normal detection path. GPS still cannot determine whether someone actually got off: notifications deliberately say **may have passed**. Walking/riding patterns, parallel roads, round trips and inaccurate saved points can affect this heuristic.

After arrival, possible-missed monitoring has a ten-minute deadline. Evaluation and reconciliation expire it on the next task/app wake even if the wake has no fresh GPS. An operating system that suspends all execution can postpone cleanup; this is not a strict native timer guarantee. Turn the alarm off once the destination is complete. Arrival-only and approach-only configurations stop after their last selected stage; a possible-missed alert ends its session.

Events use stable session/stage IDs and a persisted queue. Later stages supersede stale queued approach reminders. Delivery validates the current alarm/session before and after posting; later notifications replace the previous posted alert for that alarm. Failed deliveries stay retryable independently across alarms. Approach/arrival retries expire after two minutes and possible-missed retries after five, preventing a restarted app from ringing an old update as current.

Android stores separate immutable sound/vibration channels. **Phone alarm** follows the system alarm sound; **Choose from phone alarm sounds** references accessible OS tone URIs without copying audio. iOS cannot expose Clock's private tone list or independently toggle notification vibration. Both platforms retain imports and six original bundled cues. See [sound provenance](../assets/sounds/ADDITIONAL_TONES.md) and [local imports](LOCAL_ALARM_SOUNDS.md).

The [Clock-inspired alarm screen](ALARM_EXPERIENCE_PLAN.md) is implemented with three stage states, notification-tap and foreground opening, event-only mute, “I’m here” completion, confirmed end tracking, and a read-only destination map. Schema 7 retains these screen events separately from the delivery queue. Closing silences a cue without acknowledging or ending the session. There is no continuous ringing, lock-screen takeover, or time-based snooze. Device acceptance must cover real rides and missed passes, walking away after alighting, weak/underground GPS, OS batching, process restart, multiple alarms, both vibration choices, phone/bundled/imported sounds, removed device tones, silent/Focus/channel settings, and large-text/screen-reader use.

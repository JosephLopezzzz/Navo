# Navo route companion

Seed: `c785c651` · selected kind: assigned · build path: code · user lock: 2026-10-25

## World

A quiet commuter route guide treated like an editorial route plate: near-white paper tone, one ink voice, hairline registration marks, a single route line, numbered stops, and a small source-quality seal. The blue-to-teal Navo mark supplies the accent. The image is disciplined printed wayfinding, not a literal magazine page. Native navigation and controls remain platform-familiar.

## First viewport

The home screen asks where the rider is heading. It places rail lines as open route strips with endpoint and station counts, then offers a separate bus-route action. Setup shows the ordered stops between boarding and destination before the rider arms anything. The active trip puts the destination and current/next stop first, with a clear sequence line and a visible data-quality seal. It does not invent an ETA.

## Visitor path

Choose LRT-1, LRT-2, MRT-3, or make a bus route; select boarding and destination stops; choose every-stop or destination-only wake mode; review location, permission, and route-data readiness; start; track the next stop; confirm manually if location is unavailable; end or complete the trip.

## Signature interaction

Selecting boarding and destination cuts the line's ordered list down to the rider's exact direction. The route plate then remains the trip's progress surface: passed, next, and destination stops have distinct labels and a connected route mark. Every progression step can also be confirmed by the rider.

## Cross-surface reach

Keep the same route plate grammar for rail and rider-authored bus sequences. Use the source seal for provenance, coordinate quality, and location freshness. Use the same passed/next/destination order on setup, active trip, and lock-screen or notification surfaces where the platform supports it.

## Honest risk

The bundled rail station order comes from operator/authority pages, but this first cut has no audited station coordinates. Rail trips therefore remain manual-sequence trips until location data is verified. A bus route can use coordinates the rider records at each stop. Background updates and notifications still depend on permissions, OS policy, device conditions, and an installed development build; they are not guaranteed to wake a sleeping phone like an alarm clock. There is no ETA or live service feed.

## Grounded candidate list

1. Transit atlas: route and stop order lead; broadest wayfinding recognition, with a risk of looking like a standard transit app. Impeccable's pick.
2. Bus destination placard: route endpoints are strong and familiar, but it skews the system toward buses.
3. Editorial route plates: a quiet sequence, source mark, and clear status without promising live data. Assigned by seed and selected by the user.
4. Pocket timetable: familiar printed transit information, but columns can imply schedule data Navo does not have.
5. Concourse status board: readable fixed states, but can imply live departures and service updates.
6. Paper ticket and transfer stub: communicates direction and progression, but fare and transfer cues are outside the MVP.
7. Folded city map: gives geographic context, but a map-first surface would make unverified coordinates look authoritative.

## Challengers and verdicts

- Pocket timetable slide rack — **competitive**: strong commuter identification, weaker product clarity because its schedule structure implies times Navo does not provide. Keep compact ordered rows, not a timetable.
- Midnight-blue rail diagram — **competitive**: strong route-sequence clarity and rail recognition, weaker fit for bus journeys and destination-alert emphasis. Keep a connected, direction-aware route line.
- Split-flap concourse board — **declined**: it makes the rider expect live arrivals and service changes, which are absent. Keep fixed, readable status labels without departure-board motion.
- Acetate tabbed manual — **declined**: its physical manual metaphor has weak commuter recognition. Keep persistent step hierarchy for passed, next, and destination stops.
- Cassette j-card — **declined**: its audience and track-list task do not fit transit destination alerts. Keep temporal transparency by showing location-fix freshness, never an unsourced ETA.

The user's selected assigned direction overrides any roll preference. No challenger replaces the route-plate world.
